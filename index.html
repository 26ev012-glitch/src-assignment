Food Waste

Fresh food, saved from the bin

The Big Picture

Food is lost or thrown away at every step between the farm and the dinner table. Some of it spoils in storage or during transport, some is trimmed away during preparation, and a surprising amount is simply forgotten in the fridge until it is no longer good to eat.

It helps to separate two ideas:

  • Food loss happens early, during growing, storage, processing and delivery.
  • Food waste happens late, when food that was still fit to eat is thrown away by shops, kitchens and households.

When food rots in a landfill it gives off methane, a greenhouse gas. Every meal we save also saves the water, energy and money that went into making it.

Benefits of Less Food Waste

  • Lower grocery bills, because you buy and cook only what you will eat.
  • Less rubbish to dispose of, and fewer emissions from landfills.
  • Better use of the energy, water and land used to grow and transport food.
  • Safe, untouched food reaches neighbours and food banks instead of the bin.

The Food Recovery Hierarchy

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency ranks the ways to deal with surplus food, from the best option at the top to the last resort at the bottom:

  1. Source reduction: prepare and buy less in the first place.
  2. Feed hungry people: donate extra food to community sites.
  3. Feed animals: give scraps to farmers for animal feed.
  4. Industrial uses: turn used oils and fats into fuel.
  5. Composting: return food scraps to the soil.
  6. Landfill or incineration: the last resort.

What You Can Do at Home

  • Plan meals before you shop and keep a list.
  • Store an "Eat Me First" box at eye level in the fridge.
  • Freeze leftovers before they spoil.
  • Learn the difference between "best before" and "use by" dates.
  • Share or donate what you cannot finish.
